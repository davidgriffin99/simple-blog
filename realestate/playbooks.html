California-Specific Gotchas

CEQA: Most EV charging is categorically exempt (Class 3 or Class 32), but some cities require a CEQA checklist anyway. Ask planning department upfront — don't assume.
Design Review Boards: Santa Monica, Pasadena, Beverly Hills, Malibu all have active design review boards that add 4-8 weeks. They care about aesthetics — prepare renderings showing charger placement, landscaping screening, and lighting plans.
Fire Marshal: DC fast chargers in enclosed parking structures may trigger fire marshal review. Li-ion battery concerns are increasing post-2025. Get fire marshal sign-off in the entitlement phase, not after you've started permitting.

Why Texas / ERCOT

ERCOT is an energy-only market with no capacity payments — battery economics depend on price spikes and ancillary services. The grid is isolated (no interstate connections), which creates volatility = opportunity for storage. Post-Winter Storm Uri (2021), political support for grid reliability is strong.

Critical Path: The ERCOT Queue

This is the #1 risk. ERCOT interconnection studies are backlogged 12-18 months as of early 2026. Over 200GW of solar+storage in the queue. Projects can be killed by study results showing expensive network upgrades ($5-20M). Submit early, submit often. Secure queue position before finalizing land deals — an option agreement (not purchase) lets you walk away if the study comes back bad.

Texas-Specific Gotchas

Fire suppression: Post-Megapack fires in 2023-2024, Texas counties are increasingly requiring fire suppression systems and setback distances. Budget $200K-$500K for suppression and don't assume the county won't ask. Better to include it in the initial permit application than have it added as a condition.
Property tax: Texas has no state income tax but aggressive property tax. BESS equipment is taxable property. Apply for the 313 tax abatement (school district) before construction starts — can save $1-3M over 10 years on a 50MW project. Some counties also offer additional abatements for renewable energy.
Community opposition is growing: Battery projects were uncontroversial in Texas until 2024. Now NIMBY groups are forming, especially in suburban counties near Austin, Dallas, and Houston. Cedar Park and Georgetown have both seen organized opposition. Present at county commissioners court early — don't let opponents define the narrative.

What Makes Community Solar Different

Unlike utility-scale solar (sell to grid) or rooftop (sell to building owner), community solar sells to subscribers — residential and commercial customers who get bill credits for their share of the array. This adds a customer acquisition and management layer that pure generation projects don't have.

Common Mistakes

Lesson — subscriber churn kills economics: Our first community solar project in Illinois had 22% annual subscriber churn. At that rate, customer acquisition costs eat the margin. Now we require 12-month minimum commitments and focus on commercial subscribers (lower churn, larger allocations). Residential-heavy portfolios need a dedicated retention strategy.
Lesson — agricultural land traps: Cheap farmland seems ideal for solar until you discover it's in an agricultural preservation district. Two of our sites needed special use permits that added 4 months and required a public hearing where farmers spoke against "taking productive land." Scout for brownfields, commercial-zoned parcels, and dual-use (agrivoltaics) opportunities first.
Lesson — interconnection cost surprises: Utility quoted $150K for interconnection. Final cost: $480K after the study revealed a transformer upgrade was needed. Always budget 2-3x the initial utility estimate for interconnection. Get a detailed cost estimate in writing before committing to land.
